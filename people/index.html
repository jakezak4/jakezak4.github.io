    Principal Investigator

    Jacob Jaszczak, PhD [jay-zak]

    Jacob’s passion for biology research began at Michigan Technological University where he earned his BS degree and studied diatom biosilicification. After obtaining his Ph.D. from the University of Virginia in Cell and Developmental Biology with Dr. Adrian Halme, he began his research in neuroscience as a postdoctoral scholar with Dr. Yuh-Nung Jan at the Howard Hughes Medical Institute and University of California, San Francisco. His favorite backyard bird is the cactus wren and he enjoys biking on the local trails and hiking to find petroglyphs.

    Research Assistant

    Douglas Hooton

    I studied Drosophila population genetics during my undergraduate at UC San Diego while working in the National Drosophila Stock Center before earning a MS degree at UC Los Angeles with Dr. Stephen Hubbell studying the diversity and spatial dynamics of heart-rot fungal pathogens. I’m now excited to be working on behavioral and cellular nociceptive responses to chemical stimuli in Drosophila larvae. When not in the lab, I spend much of my time playing with my dogs, Rudy and Benny, or running the trails in the Organ and Franklin Mountains.

    PhD student

    Jannatul Mahmuda

    I completed my undergraduate and master’s degrees in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at Shahjalal University of Science and Technology in Bangladesh. During my master’s research, I worked with hypertensive pregnant women, investigating the relationship between C-reactive protein (CRP) levels and hypertension. I later earned a second master’s degree from the Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry at New Mexico State University (NMSU), where I gained research experience in cancer biology. Currently, I am pursuing my PhD in the Interdisciplinary Molecular Biology program at NMSU. My research focuses on understanding the effects of chemotherapeutic agents on the sensory nervous system. Outside the lab, I enjoy traveling to new places, gardening, reading and seeking out new experiences. I’m always curious to explore whether it’s a new scientific question, a new destination, or simply something I’ve never tried before.
